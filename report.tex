\begin{abstract}
This report converts the approved CrashGuard proposal into an ESP32 prototype
with a repeatable experimental workflow while separating measurements from
assumptions. The system
uses a handlebar-mounted MPU-6050, an ESP32 DevKit V1, a local light and
sound alarm, a cancel button, and an optional Blynk event sent over a phone
hotspot. No physical hardware was available for this review. Consequently,
the evidence consists of source-level unit tests, exact C++ replay of the
firmware detector over a deterministic physics-informed dataset, an audit of
an independent powered-two-wheeler dataset, diagram validation, and an
executable Wokwi project. The generated evaluation contains
\SimulationEvents{} ten-second events, or \SimulationSamples{} six-axis
samples at 100 Hz. CrashGuard v2 produced \VTwoTP{} true positives,
\VTwoTN{} true negatives, \VTwoFP{} false positives, and \VTwoFN{} false
negatives: sensitivity \VTwoSensitivity{} and specificity
\VTwoSpecificity{}. These are simulation results, not claims of road-crash
performance. The original Review-1 rule was too strict, and the supplied
base paper contains reproducibility and numerical inconsistencies. Both are
therefore treated as inputs to be tested, not final truth. All firmware,
Wokwi wiring, scenarios, dataset acquisition and generation scripts, recorded
hashes, analysis code, derived results, and paper sources are delivered with
this report.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Research integrity statement.}
No physical test, Wokwi cloud run, battery-runtime measurement, network
delivery measurement, or real road crash is represented as completed. A
Wokwi CI token is an external credential and is not committed. The reported
classification results come only from deterministic replay and are labelled
accordingly.

\section{Problem, scope, and contributions}

India recorded 172,890 road deaths in 2023; 77,539, or 44.8\%, were users of
two-wheelers according to the latest detailed MoRTH report available at the
time of this review~\cite{morth2023}. CrashGuard addresses one narrow part of
this problem: reducing the interval between an inertial event and an alert.
It does not prevent a crash, assess injury, guarantee communication, or
replace emergency services.

The technical question is whether a low-cost, vehicle-mounted six-axis IMU
can reject common high-dynamic riding events while retaining sensitivity to
fall-like signatures. Three research questions guide the work:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{RQ\arabic*:}]
  \item Does temporal correlation of impact, rotation, and persistent rest
        reduce false triggers relative to an acceleration-only rule?
  \item Do the original Review-1 thresholds survive comparison with an
        independent powered-two-wheeler dataset?
  \item Can the same production C++ decision logic execute on an ESP32,
        under host replay, and in Wokwi without a simulation-only sensor
        branch?
\end{enumerate}

The contribution is not a claim of a finished safety product. It is a
falsifiable prototype package with: (i) a corrected electrical design,
(ii) a state-machine detector with centralized parameters, (iii) a public
dataset audit, (iv) a deterministic robustness dataset, (v) three algorithm
comparisons, and (vi) automated firmware-level Wokwi scenarios.

\section{Critical audit of the supplied base paper}
\label{sec:baseaudit}

The supplied paper by Tian et al.~\cite{tian2026transformer} is a useful
methodological reference, but it solves a different sensing problem. Its
device is attached to a person's shin, samples an LSM6DSM at 50 Hz, segments
170-sample windows, and classifies human activities and falls with a
CNN-transformer. CrashGuard is fixed to a motorcycle handlebar, samples an
MPU-6050 at 100 Hz, and detects vehicle dynamics. Mounting location changes
the signal-generating body, reference orientation, vibration spectrum, and
meaning of a fall label. Directly copying its network or its metrics would
therefore be a category error.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{What is transferred from the base paper and what is not.}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{}p{0.24\textwidth}XX@{}}
\toprule
Aspect & Valid lesson & CrashGuard decision \\
\midrule
Six-axis time series & Acceleration alone does not encode the temporal
sequence adequately & Correlate acceleration, angular rate, orientation,
and post-event rest \\
Class imbalance & Accuracy can hide misses when the minority event is rare
& Report TP, TN, FP, FN, sensitivity, specificity, precision, and F1 \\
Long temporal context & Pre-event and post-event evidence matter & Use a
small deterministic state machine instead of a 3.4 s tensor model \\
Hardware co-design & Model choice must respect memory, power, and latency &
Use constant-memory arithmetic suitable for the ESP32 \\
Reported human-fall score & Useful only for its stated dataset and placement
& Do not use 93.1\% as motorcycle evidence \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The paper and accompanying archive were audited rather than accepted at
face value. Four issues matter for replication:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item It reports an ROC operating point $({\rm FPR},{\rm TPR})=(0.055,
        0.905)$ and AUC 0.768. Any nondecreasing ROC curve passing through
        that point has the lower bound
        \begin{equation}
          {\rm AUC}\geq {\rm TPR}(1-{\rm FPR})
          =0.905(1-0.055)=0.855,
        \end{equation}
        even if it contributes no area before the point. The published AUC
        and operating point cannot both describe the same valid ROC curve.
  \item It calls the deployment representation both ``8-bit'' and
        ``Q7.8''. Fixed-point Q notation varies, but Q7.8 requires more than
        eight total bits under conventional definitions. An exact sign,
        integer, fraction, saturation, and rounding specification is absent.
  \item The paper reports 9,195 and 42,351 windows, class ratios,
        augmentation, and subject-level evaluation. The downloaded
        HealthLINK archive contains raw recording files but no complete
        machine-readable label, window, split, and augmentation manifest
        sufficient to reconstruct those two tensors exactly.
  \item Its 502 kB reported model footprint, FPGA-specific resource claims,
        and human-body placement do not establish feasibility or validity on
        an ESP32 handlebar unit.
\end{enumerate}

The archive is retained and hashed for provenance, but is not mixed into the
motorcycle evaluation. This is a deliberate exclusion based on domain and
label integrity, not an attempt to discard inconvenient data.

\section{Related research and design position}

The strongest directly relevant lineage is Boubezoul et al. Their 2013
detector used powered-two-wheeler dynamics~\cite{boubezoul2013algorithm}, and
their later data article released four controlled fall recordings and seven
critical or extreme manoeuvre recordings~\cite{boubezoul2019dataset}.
Gelmini et al. developed and experimentally validated vehicle-mounted crash
logic and later a novel two-wheeler algorithm~\cite{gelmini2019validation,
gelmini2021novel}. Mahariba et al. used the Boubezoul data for a machine
learning study~\cite{mahariba2022ml}. Lee et al. compared threshold,
supervised, and unsupervised IMU approaches for e-scooter pre-impact fall
detection~\cite{lee2024escooter}. The 2026 VZCrash release is much larger,
with real fleet crashes, but its commercial four-wheel vehicle population
does not remove the powered-two-wheeler domain gap~\cite{bianconcini2026vzcrash}.

The literature does not justify selecting a complex model merely because one
is available. A supervised network would require a sufficiently large,
representative, labelled dataset from the target motorcycle placement and a
subject or vehicle independent split. That dataset is not available here.
The current rule is transparent, constant-memory, testable sample by sample,
and appropriate for the evidence level. ML remains a later comparison after
physical data collection.

No display was proposed or added: local status is conveyed by LED and sound,
while event history appears in Blynk. Adding a display solely to satisfy a
generic checklist would increase load and weather sealing complexity without
serving the approved architecture.

The MPU-6050 is configured to $\pm16g$ and
$\pm2000\degree\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}$ so impact and rapid rotation do not
immediately saturate the narrower default ranges~\cite{mpu6050datasheet}.
The 100 nF sensor capacitor and 100 $\mu$F rail capacitor reduce local supply
transients. GPIO34 is input-only, which is appropriate for the divider, and
its ADC1 assignment avoids the ESP32 ADC2 and Wi-Fi conflict documented for
the platform~\cite{esp32datasheet}.

The audible part is a 5 V passive piezo transducer rated at no more than
30 mA. GPIO25 supplies a 2.4 kHz waveform through a 1 k$\Omega$ base resistor;
the 2N2222 switches transducer current and a 10 k$\Omega$ resistor keeps it
off during boot. A magnetic sounder would require a flyback diode and is not
interchangeable with the specified piezo part.

The TP4056/DW01A module is a charger with cell protection, not a true power
path. The specified circuit must be switched off while charging. A future
simultaneous charge-and-run version must replace it with a load-sharing power
path charger. This corrects a common but unsafe schematic shortcut.

\subsection{Analytic power budget, not a measurement}

An engineering estimate uses a 5 V average rail current of about 120 mA in
connected standby. Thus $P_{5V}\approx0.60$ W. At 3.7 V and assumed 90\%
boost efficiency,
\begin{equation}
 I_{cell}\approx\frac{0.60}{3.7(0.90)}=0.180\ \mathrm{A}.
\end{equation}
If 90\% of a 2600 mAh cell is usable, the idealized runtime is
$2.34/0.180\approx13$ h. ESP32 radio duty cycle, converter efficiency,
cell age, temperature, alarm duty, and regulator loss can shift this result
substantially. It is a sizing calculation awaiting a logged discharge test.

The external 1 kHz PTW channels are resampled to 100 Hz with a polyphase
anti-alias filter before feature extraction. The four fall time stamps are
taken from the dataset article; a three-second interval centered on each is
analysed. Complete manoeuvre recordings are retained as negatives. The
published sensor ranges are narrower than the MPU-6050 configuration, and
the acceleration appears unsuitable as a persistent raw gravity reference.
For that reason the external audit tests only impact and rotation gates, not
the complete tilted-rest state machine.

\subsection{Physics-informed generated dataset}

Each event lasts 10 s and uses seed \SimulationSeed. The generator constructs
roll $\phi(t)$, pitch $\theta(t)$, yaw $\psi(t)$, body-frame gravity, linear
manoeuvre pulses, sensor bias, and Gaussian noise. With roll and pitch, the
ideal gravity vector in sensor coordinates is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf{g}_b(t)=
 \begin{bmatrix}
 -\sin\theta(t)\\
 \sin\phi(t)\cos\theta(t)\\
 \cos\phi(t)\cos\theta(t)
 \end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
Linear components are added for potholes, bumps, braking, turning, impact,
vertical unloading, and post-impact ringing. Angular rates are numerical
derivatives of the attitude trajectories. Finally, a per-event bias and
sample noise are added and values are clipped to $\pm16g$ and
$\pm2000\degree/\mathrm{s}$. This is more realistic than arbitrary threshold
crossings, but it is still a model whose assumptions may favor the proposed
logic.

Nine balanced-within-scenario groups contain 75 events each: normal riding,
pothole, speed breaker, hard brake, sharp turn, parked tip-over, impact then
recovery, low-side crash, and high-side crash. Only the last two are positive.
There is no training set because no statistical model is fitted. Parameters
are read directly from the C++ header, and the evaluation script does not
optimize them. Nevertheless, threshold selection and simulator development
occurred in the same project, so the resulting scores are an exploratory
robustness check, not an independent generalization estimate.

The gzip timestamp is fixed to zero and its embedded filename is empty. The
compressed file is therefore byte-reproducible, not merely value-reproducible.
SHA-256 provenance can be checked with:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash,numbers=none]
sha256sum -c data/manifest.sha256
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Mathematical decision model}
\label{sec:model}

For raw signed values $r_a,r_\omega$ under the selected full-scale ranges,
firmware conversion is
\begin{equation}
 a_i=\frac{r_{a,i}}{2048}\ g,\qquad
 \omega_i=\frac{r_{\omega,i}}{16.4}\ \degree/\mathrm{s}.
\end{equation}
At each 10 ms sample, resultant acceleration and angular rate are
\begin{equation}
 A_k=\sqrt{a_x^2+a_y^2+a_z^2},\qquad
 \Omega_k=\sqrt{\omega_x^2+\omega_y^2+\omega_z^2}.
\end{equation}
During a stationary two-second startup calibration, 200 samples establish
the reference gravity vector $\mathbf{r}$. Calibration is rejected unless
$0.85\leq\|\mathbf{r}\|\leq1.15g$ and gyroscope RMS is at most
$5\degree/\mathrm{s}$. Tilt relative to the installed upright pose is
\begin{equation}
 \Theta_k=\cos^{-1}\!\left(
 \operatorname{clip}\frac{\mathbf{a}_k\cdot\mathbf{r}}
 {\|\mathbf{a}_k\|\|\mathbf{r}\|},-1,1\right).
\end{equation}

\subsection{Activity gate}

A parked motorcycle can be knocked over and should not automatically generate
a remote alert. Define sample activity as
\begin{equation}
 U_k=[|A_k-1|\geq0.08]\lor[\Omega_k\geq4].
\end{equation}
A leaky evidence accumulator increases by $\Delta t$ when $U_k$ is true and
decays by $\Delta t/2$ otherwise, clipped to $[0,1000]$ ms. Reaching 1000 ms
qualifies a ride and opens a 5000 ms rolling activity memory. This heuristic
does not prove that the engine is running; it only prevents a single isolated
parked tip from qualifying itself.

\subsection{State rules}

The parameter values below are compiled into both firmware and replay code.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\small
\caption{CrashGuard v2 decision parameters.}
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
Parameter & Value & Meaning \\
\midrule
$A_H$ & \VTwoImpactThreshold{} g & High-impact threshold \\
$A_L$ & \VTwoLowImpactThreshold{} g & Unloading or near-free-fall threshold \\
$\Omega_T$ & \VTwoRotationThreshold{} $\degree$/s & Rotation threshold \\
$\Theta_T$ & \VTwoTiltThreshold{}$\degree$ & Final tilt threshold \\
$[A_{R,L},A_{R,H}]$ & $[0.70,1.30]$ g & Rest acceleration band \\
$\Omega_R$ & 30 $\degree$/s & Maximum angular rate at rest \\
$T_C$ & \VTwoCorrelationMs{} ms & Impact-rotation correlation window \\
$T_R$ & \VTwoRestHoldMs{} ms & Continuous tilted-rest hold \\
$T_V$ & 6000 ms & Candidate verification timeout \\
$T_X$ & 15000 ms & Rider cancel interval \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For an activity-qualified sample, an impact candidate is
\begin{equation}
 H_k=[A_k\geq A_H]\lor[A_k\leq A_L].
\end{equation}
Rotation may precede or follow impact. The detector accepts the pair when
$H_k$ occurs within $T_C$ of a previous $\Omega\geq\Omega_T$, or a qualifying
rotation occurs within $T_C$ after $H_k$. This symmetric correlation corrects
the Review-1 assumption that impact must always arrive first.

The final evidence is continuous tilted rest:
\begin{equation}
 R_k=[\Theta_k\geq\Theta_T]\land
 [0.70\leq A_k\leq1.30]\land[\Omega_k\leq30].
\end{equation}
Only if $R_k$ remains true for $T_R$ does the system enter the countdown.
Failure to establish rest by $T_V$ rejects the candidate. Countdown expiry
produces one remote-event attempt; pressing CANCEL returns to idle. All time
subtractions use unsigned wraparound-safe arithmetic.

\section{Baseline, original method, and proposed algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithms}

The comparison deliberately includes both a weak baseline and the actual
Review-1 proposal.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2em,labelindent=0pt]
  \item[Acceleration-only baseline:] classify an event positive if any
        $A_k\geq2g$. It is fast and sensitive but ignores context.
  \item[Review-1 ordered logic:] require $A_k\geq4g$, then
        $\Omega_k\geq200\degree/\mathrm{s}$ within 500 ms, then
        $\Theta_k\geq60\degree$ with $0.7\leq A_k\leq1.3g$ for 3 s.
  \item[CrashGuard v2:] activity gating, high or low acceleration,
        symmetric temporal correlation with rotation, calibrated 3D tilt,
        two-second rest verification, and local cancellation.
\end{description}

\begin{lstlisting}[caption={Executable-level pseudocode for CrashGuard v2.},label={lst:pseudo}]
sample MPU6050 at 100 Hz
compute acceleration magnitude, angular-rate magnitude, and calibrated tilt
update leaky ride-activity evidence

IDLE:
  if ride memory is active and (A >= 2.0 g or A <= 0.35 g):
    open candidate
    if rotation >= 90 deg/s occurred in the previous 750 ms:
      go to VERIFY_REST
    else:
      go to CORRELATE
CORRELATE:
  if rotation >= 90 deg/s within 750 ms: go to VERIFY_REST
  else if 750 ms expires: reject and return to IDLE
VERIFY_REST:
  require tilt >= 55 deg, 0.70 <= A <= 1.30 g,
          and rotation <= 30 deg/s continuously for 2000 ms
  if satisfied: sound local alarm and start 15000 ms countdown
  if 6000 ms candidate timeout expires: reject
COUNTDOWN:
  if CANCEL is pressed: silence and return to IDLE
  if countdown expires: send one Blynk event and enter ALERT_SENT
\end{lstlisting}

\section{Implementation and software verification}
\label{sec:software}

\code{wokwi/sketch.ino} is production-oriented code. It configures the
MPU-6050 registers directly, uses 400 kHz I\textsuperscript{2}C, maintains a
100 Hz deadline, calibrates gyroscope bias and gravity reference, debounces an
active-low button edge, drives the local alarm, reads the protected-cell
divider, and optionally calls Blynk over HTTPS. It has no conditional branch
that injects synthetic sensor values.

The decision implementation lives in \code{wokwi/crash\_detector.h} and has
no Arduino dependency. \code{analysis/detector\_replay.cpp} includes that
same header, ensuring that reported v2 decisions are made by the deployment
logic rather than a Python reimplementation. Four native unit tests cover
rotation before impact, pothole rejection, parked tip rejection, and cancel.
The complete pipeline compiles the tests with warnings as errors, runs them,
generates the dataset, streams the gzip CSV through the C++ replay program,
calculates metrics, audits external data, and renders the PDF figures.

An ESP32 Arduino core 3.3.11 target compile with all compiler warnings enabled
succeeds. The offline firmware image uses 392,947 bytes, or 29\% of the
1,310,720-byte application partition, and statically allocated globals use
29,808 bytes, or 9\% of 327,680 bytes. These compiler figures establish build
feasibility but do not measure maximum stack, heap fragmentation, timing, or
energy on a running board. Arduino CLI requires the primary \code{.ino} name
to match its directory, so a four-line adapter under
\code{wokwi/arduino-build/sketch/} includes the root Wokwi
\code{sketch.ino}; it does not duplicate firmware logic.

Cloud credentials are held in an optional untracked \code{secrets.h}. The
offline build remains fully functional. Failed Wi-Fi or HTTPS delivery does
not block sampling. A production revision should add a persistent outbound
queue, delivery acknowledgement, TLS trust-store policy, watchdog recovery,
and rate limiting.

CrashGuard v2 sensitivity has Wilson 95\% interval

The acceleration-only rule demonstrates the expected tradeoff: high
sensitivity but 289 false triggers, mostly high-dynamic road events. The
Review-1 rule has no false positives but misses 111 of 150 simulated crashes.
V2 recovers 75 of those missed positives without adding a false positive in
this generated set. All 75 high-side events are detected; 39 of 75 lower
dynamic low-side events are detected. These 36 low-side misses are the main
known failure mode and make the original 90\% detection objective unmet at
this evidence stage.

\section{Validity, safety, privacy, and limitations}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Simulation-to-road gap.} Generated trajectories omit frame
        flex, mounting slip, engine harmonics, impacts at varied locations,
        temperature drift, sensor clipping details, and rider-vehicle
        separation. Scores cannot be promoted as field accuracy.
  \item \textbf{Dataset dependence.} The external PTW set has only four
        controlled falls. HealthLINK has the wrong sensing domain, and its
        paper-level processing could not be exactly reconstructed.
  \item \textbf{Threshold development bias.} The simulation generator and
        detector were developed in the same project. No blinded physical
        holdout has been evaluated.
  \item \textbf{No measured non-detection exposure.} Event specificity is
        not a false-alert rate per hour or kilometre. A road exposure study is
        required for the operational objective.
  \item \textbf{Communication.} A hotspot may be disabled, out of coverage,
        or disconnected. HTTPS success is not emergency-contact receipt.
  \item \textbf{Location and privacy.} No coordinates are fabricated. Adding
        GNSS or a companion application creates consent, retention, and access
        control requirements.
  \item \textbf{Safety.} Intentional public-road crash tests are prohibited.
        Future controlled tip-over work must use an unmanned frame, remote
        release, soft containment, cell protection, and responsible lab
        supervision.
\end{enumerate}

This prototype is not a certified emergency, medical, or automotive safety
device. A false negative may prevent an alert and a false positive may alarm
contacts. The local cancel interval is a usability control, not a guarantee.

\section{Conclusion and next validation stage}

The implementation satisfies the Review-2 deliverables at simulation level
and exposes rather than hides what remains unknown. The original 4 g,
200 $\degree$/s ordered method does not survive the public PTW audit and has
only \LegacySensitivity{} sensitivity on generated events. The proposed
activity-gated symmetric state machine raises simulated sensitivity to
\VTwoSensitivity{}, keeps zero observed false positives in 525 generated
negative events, and uses the exact code deployed in the ESP32 sketch. It
still misses 36 lower-dynamic events and has no road-valid accuracy claim.

The next valid experiment is staged, not a real crash. First, verify rail
voltages, sensor orientation, buzzer transistor current, charge-only
interlock, and sampling jitter on the bench. Second, collect labelled
stationary tip-overs and suspended-frame drops onto a soft mat. Third, log at
least 300 km of ordinary riding across potholes, bumps, braking, turns, and
mounting variations. Freeze thresholds before a held-out route, report alerts
per hour and per kilometre with confidence bounds, and compare against a
small interpretable ML model only after that target-domain dataset exists.
